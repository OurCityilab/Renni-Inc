# Rose City Marketplace Game Rules

Rose City Marketplace is a housing, credit, rent, and ownership simulation.

## Player count

Works with 5–20 students. Use NPC properties and NPC landlords when student count is low.

## Learning objectives

Students should understand income, credit, debt-to-income ratio, car affordability, rent affordability, mortgage qualification, security deposits, down payments, landlord screening, supply/demand, market competition, asset ownership, cash flow, and housing affordability.

## Round structure

### Round 1: Life Profile

Each student receives job, median salary, monthly gross income, estimated take-home pay, credit score, savings, debt, and monthly obligations. The system calculates DTI, max car payment, max rent, and estimated mortgage buying power.

### Round 2: Transportation

Students choose no car, cash car, used car loan, newer used car loan, or public/shared transportation. Transportation affects monthly payment, insurance, maintenance, housing affordability, and savings left.

### Round 3: Rental Market

Students apply for rental properties. Landlords, student or NPC, can set rent/deposit/minimum credit, approve, reject, ask for higher deposit, or lower rent to fill vacancy.

### Round 4: Home Buying

Qualified students bid on homes. Home cards include purchase price, required down payment, estimated payment, taxes, insurance, repair reserve, expected rent, expected resale value, and risk rating.

### Round 5: Landlord Mode

Students who own property can rent units to others. Landlord decisions include rent, deposit, minimum credit score, risk tolerance, renovation decision, and reserve amount.

### Round 6: Event Cards

Events may affect income, credit, expenses, rent, interest rates, repairs, vacancy, taxes, and insurance.

### Round 7: Recalculate and Reflect

Students update monthly cash flow, housing status, savings, debt, and net worth.

## AI coaching moments

After rejection: Why do you think you were rejected? What number hurt you most? What could you improve next round?

After approval: Are you approved and comfortable, or only approved? What happens if a surprise expense hits?

After landlord decision: Did you price for profit, fairness, or market demand? What is your vacancy risk? Is this tenant risky or stable?

## Student report

Each student receives a Money Report with starting profile, decisions, results, what helped, what hurt, what they learned, and next move.
