# Rose City Market Day Game Rules

Rose City Market Day is a traditional goods-and-services marketplace simulation.

Every student is both a business owner and a consumer with a budget.

## Player count

Works with 5–20 students. If fewer students are present, NPC stores fill the market.

## Learning objectives

Students should understand revenue vs. profit, costs, pricing, quality, customer choice, competition, market demand, inventory, capacity, customer satisfaction, P&L, and KPIs.

## Business categories

Goods: apparel, snacks/baked goods, candles/scents, phone accessories, art/prints, jewelry/accessories, school supplies, wellness products, beauty/skincare, custom merch.

Services: hair/braiding/barber service, tutoring, car wash/detailing, lawn care, photography, social media/content creation, event setup, fitness coaching, resume/LinkedIn help, tech support.

## Round structure

### Round 1: Build Your Business

Students choose business name, product/service, target customer, customer problem, and value proposition.

### Round 2: Choose Inputs and Quality

Quality tiers: Budget inputs: low cost/lower quality; Standard inputs: moderate cost/reliable quality; Premium inputs: high cost/high quality.

### Round 3: Set Price and Capacity

Students choose price, inventory/capacity, marketing spend, and optional bundle/discount strategy. System calculates cost per unit, total cost, break-even quantity, and gross margin per unit.

### Round 4: 30-Second Pitch

Students pitch what they sell, who it is for, why it is worth the price, and why classmates should buy.

### Round 5: Buying Round 1

Students spend personal budgets. They cannot buy from themselves. They can buy from classmates, NPC stores, or save money. They should explain purchase choices.

### Round 6: Market Event

Events include supplier cost increase, premium materials out of stock, NPC sale, trend shift, viral boost, bad review, booth fee increase, weather impact, or demand surge.

### Round 7: Adjust Strategy

Students may keep price, raise price, discount, improve quality, reduce quality, buy more inventory, change marketing, or bundle products.

### Round 8: Buying Round 2

Students shop again.

### Round 9: Close the Books

Students calculate revenue, COGS, gross profit, operating expenses, net profit, profit margin, units sold, inventory left/capacity used, and customer satisfaction.

### Round 10: Present KPIs and P&L

Students present business strategy, KPIs, P&L, what worked, what did not, and what they would change.

## NPC stores

NPC competitors: Budget Barn, Main Street Standard, Premium House, Convenience Corner, Trendy Pop-Up.

## Required KPIs

Units sold, revenue, gross profit, net profit, profit margin, conversion rate, customer satisfaction, inventory left or capacity used.

## Scoring

Do not score only on revenue. Suggested scoreboard: Net Profit 25, Customer Satisfaction 20, Profit Margin 15, Units Sold 15, Smart Pricing Strategy 10, Adaptation After Market Event 10, Final Presentation 5.
