## SLIDE 5 — DAY 1: THE OFFER
**$15.50/hour · 21 hours/week · 6 weeks (GDYT summer)**
Write down — no calculator: how much of that is YOURS?
> Collect gut guesses on the board before any math.

## SLIDE 6 — MINI-LESSON: TWO NUMBERS
- **Gross pay** — what the job pays
- **Take-home** — what hits your account (**≈75%** of gross)
- The difference: federal + state taxes, FICA (Social Security & Medicare)
> One pass, plain language. Nobody stole from you — but plan around the wrong number and the gap comes out of your plans.

## SLIDE 7 — THE MATH
$15.50 × 21 × 6 = **$1,953 gross**
× 0.75 ≈ **$1,465 take-home**
≈ **$244/week**
> Compare to their guesses. The gap between guess and reality is today's lesson.

## SLIDE 10 — DAY 2: $1,300. GO.
The 1st of the month. **$1,300** take-home. You cover: transportation, phone, food, clothes, helping at home, fun, savings.
**Total must equal $1,300. Something always gives.**
> Budget round 1, 40 minutes, packet grid.

## SLIDE 11 — MINI-LESSON: PROTECT → PLAN → PLAY
- **Protect** essentials first
- **Plan** your goals second
- **Play** with what remains — fun gets a real line, or the budget is fiction
> A budget with no joy is a budget you'll quit by week two.

## SLIDE 12 — INCOMING.
**Event card #1.** Rebudget. Still $1,300.
*(later)* **Event card #2.** Rebudget again.
Write one sentence: what gave, and why.
> Two shocks, not one. One shock teaches adjustment; two teach systems.

## SLIDE 13 — GALLERY WALK + DEFENSE
Post your budget. Read everyone else's. One sticky note each: something smart, or a risk they're missing.
Then 60 seconds: **what we protected · what we cut · what $200 more would change**
> Enforce student-safe feedback language.

## SLIDE 15 — DAY 3: "4 EASY PAYMENTS OF $45"
$180 sneakers. No credit check. Get them today.
**What's the catch? There's always a catch. Find it.**
> Late fees, autopay overdrafts, the habit, and $180 you don't have is still $180 you don't have.

## SLIDE 16 — MINI-LESSON: FIVE WORDS THAT RUN YOUR FILE
- **Interest** — rent on money
- **Minimum payment** — designed to keep you paying
- **Payment history** — the heaviest factor; boring on-time wins
- **Utilization** — maxed limit looks desperate, even paid
- **Debt-to-income** — tomorrow's landlord math
> One sentence each. Depth comes from the cases.

## SLIDE 17 — THE TOURNAMENT
Four cases: **Sneaker Split · Secured Card · The Lot on the Corner · The Emergency**
Verdict each: **SMART / RISKY / TRAP** — prove it with a number.
> Case C's number: $4,500 car at 24%/48mo ≈ $7,100 total. Case D has no clean answer — score reasoning.

## SLIDE 18 — THE MINIMUM PAYMENT TRAP
$600 balance · 27% interest · $20 minimum
Guess: how long? how much total?
**≈ 4 years. ≈ $960.** Same debt at $60/month: **≈ 11 months, ≈ $80 interest.**
> Highest-impact 20 minutes of the week. Let the room react before you show the $60 line.

## SLIDE 19 — DEBATE: IS CREDIT BAD?
You don't pick your side — you're assigned. Numbers, not vibes.
Then everyone drops the act and we write the real answer:
**Credit isn't good or bad. Structure is.**
> End with Rewrite the Rule + the 5-rule Credit Rulebook.

## SLIDE 21 — DAY 4: THE LISTING
*1BR · $950/mo · first month + deposit at signing · income 3× rent · credit check required*
You make $16/hour full-time. **Can you even apply?**
> The math: $2,773 gross/mo vs. $2,850 required. Missed by $77. Let it hang.

## SLIDE 22 — MINI-LESSON: WHAT THE LANDLORD SEES
Income 3× rent · credit history · deposit cash · past evictions
**Thin file? The tools:** co-signer · bigger deposit · pay-history letter
> Workarounds taught as tools, not shame. "What makes this a yes in 6 months?"

## SLIDE 23 — FOUR PATHS
1. **Stay home and stack**
2. **Roommates**
3. **Move too fast**
4. **The alternative** — raise your income first (car, certification, business)
Your team builds one. Then you argue for the one you'd actually pick.
> Patient paths are strategy, not failure — say it early.
