# Market Fit Builder Framework

Market Fit Builder helps students decide who a product is actually for. It should compare possible markets using product facts, price point, quality, story, purchasing power, reachability, willingness to pay, evidence strength, and channel fit.

It must not assume Renaissance students are always the primary market. School can be the primary market, launch market, awareness market, referral market, validation market, or a weaker buying market depending on the inputs.

## Purpose

Market Fit Builder should help students:

- Compare possible customer segments.
- Understand whether product facts match the assumed customer.
- Test whether price and quality fit the market.
- Connect demand assumptions to evidence.
- Identify the best channel for each market.
- Produce Playbook-ready market positioning.

## Target Chapters

Primary V1 chapters:

- Ch. 7: Current Product Line and Pricing
- Ch. 8: Finance and Revenue Model
- Ch. 10: Pop-up Campaign and Customer Insights
- Ch. 11: Phoenix Nest Retail Carry Pitch

Later support can extend to Ch. 4 and Ch. 6 if the section needs market/customer assumption support.

## Product Facts

The builder should start with facts students can prove or inspect:

- Product name.
- Product type.
- Materials or ingredients.
- Production method.
- Quality level.
- Local production story.
- Unit cost or cost drivers.
- Proposed price.
- Available quantity.
- Production constraints.
- Brand identity cues.
- Planned sales channel.

The builder should separate product facts from student opinions.

## Segment Comparison

Students should be able to compare these possible markets:

- Renaissance students
- Parents/families
- Alumni
- Staff
- Detroit supporters
- TechTown shoppers
- Gift buyers
- Premium apparel buyers
- Custom segment

Each segment should be scored or described against:

- Fit with product story.
- Fit with price point.
- Purchasing power.
- Reachability.
- Willingness to pay.
- Channel fit.
- Evidence strength.
- Main risk.
- Next validation step.

## Comparable Product Research

Comparable products help students avoid guessing.

Students should look for:

- Similar product type.
- Similar quality level.
- Similar production story.
- Similar brand position.
- Similar customer.
- Similar sales channel.
- Price range.
- What makes the Renni product different.

Comparable products do not prove demand by themselves. They help students form a better assumption.

## Evidence Requests

Market Fit Builder should ask for evidence from:

- Student surveys.
- Interviews.
- Census or ACS income and age data.
- School-provided enrollment data.
- Comparable products.
- Retail observation.
- Phoenix Nest stakeholder feedback.
- TechTown pop-up feedback.

The builder should ask, "What evidence do we have?" before asking, "What do we believe?"

## Scenario Assumptions

Market Fit Builder should connect to scenario reasoning without replacing Market Builder calculations.

Useful scenario inputs:

- Reachable audience.
- Interest rate.
- Conversion rate.
- Price.
- Evidence strength.
- Primary channel.
- Inventory constraint.

Scenario outputs should remain estimates and should be labeled as assumptions.

## Tradeoff Summary

Each market recommendation should include:

- Best-fit segment.
- Why this segment fits.
- What makes the segment risky.
- Whether school is a buyer, launch audience, referral source, validation source, or weaker buying market.
- What evidence supports the choice.
- What evidence is missing.
- What students should validate next.

## Recommended Outputs

Market Fit Builder should produce:

- Segment comparison summary.
- Primary market recommendation.
- Secondary market or awareness market recommendation.
- Evidence strength notes.
- Weakest assumption.
- Next validation step.
- Playbook-ready positioning draft support.

The output should support student writing, not replace it.

## Student-Facing Helper Language

Use direct prompts:

- "Do not assume the first audience is the buying audience."
- "A market can be easy to reach but weak at paying."
- "A market can love the story but reject the price."
- "School might be the launch audience, not the main buyer."
- "Use evidence before calling a segment primary."
- "If the evidence is thin, mark this as an assumption and validate it."

## Example: $100 House Phoenix Sweatshirt

Product assumption:

- 100% Detroit-made House Phoenix sweatshirt.
- Premium material and local production.
- Proposed price: $100.
- Story: local pride, student enterprise, Detroit-made quality.

Market Fit Builder should question whether Renaissance students are the primary paying market.

Possible segment reading:

- Renaissance students may be strong for awareness, school pride, feedback, and launch energy.
- Students may be weaker as the primary buyer if the $100 price exceeds regular spending power.
- Parents/families may have stronger gift-buying potential.
- Alumni may value school identity and student enterprise.
- Detroit supporters may value local production and mission.
- Premium apparel buyers may value quality, but they need a retail-level brand and proof of quality.
- TechTown shoppers may be reachable during the pop-up, but evidence is needed before assuming strong conversion.

Playbook-ready insight:

"The $100 Detroit-made sweatshirt should be tested as a premium local product, not only as a school-spirit item. Renaissance students may be the strongest awareness and validation audience, while parents, alumni, Detroit supporters, and gift buyers may be stronger purchase targets. The team needs interview and pop-up feedback before naming the primary market."

## Example Contrasts

$20 t-shirt:

- More accessible to students.
- Easier impulse purchase.
- Lower margin risk depends on cost.
- School may be a stronger primary market.
- Premium positioning may be harder unless design and story are strong.

Baked goods:

- Lower price point.
- Strong impulse potential.
- Channel and freshness matter.
- TechTown shoppers and school community may both fit.
- Evidence should focus on flavor, price tolerance, and repeat purchase.

Donations:

- Not a product purchase.
- Motivation comes from mission, trust, and clarity of impact.
- Parents, alumni, staff, and supporters may be stronger than students.
- Evidence should focus on willingness to support the student enterprise and what message earns trust.

## V1 Boundaries

Market Fit Builder V1 should not:

- Import external data automatically.
- Decide the final market.
- Create final Playbook text automatically.
- Require builder completion for submit.
- Change Playbook readiness.
- Change approval status.
- Become a sales or payment tool.
