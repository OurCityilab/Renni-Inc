# Builder Interconnection Map

Market Fit Builder and Brand Fit Builder should inform each other. The strongest student work will show how product, brand, price, market, channel, evidence, and revenue assumptions fit together.

## Shared Logic

The builders should connect these questions:

- What are we selling?
- What does it look and feel like?
- What does it cost?
- Who does it attract?
- Who can pay for it?
- Where can we reach them?
- What evidence supports that fit?
- What should change if the evidence is weak?

## Market Fit Informs Brand Fit

Market Fit Builder should give Brand Fit Builder a clear target:

- Primary segment.
- Secondary or awareness segment.
- Price tolerance.
- Channel.
- Comparable products.
- Evidence strength.
- Weakest assumption.

Brand Fit Builder should then ask whether the identity supports that market.

Example:

If the market target is students with limited purchasing power, Brand Fit Builder should question whether the identity feels too premium, quiet, or expensive.

## Brand Fit Informs Market Fit

Brand Fit Builder should give Market Fit Builder a signal read:

- Who the identity seems to attract.
- What price point the identity suggests.
- What customer may feel excluded.
- What channel the identity fits.
- What proof is needed before finalizing the audience.

Market Fit Builder should then ask whether the stated market matches the brand signal.

Example:

If the brand direction feels premium and adult-facing, Market Fit Builder should question whether students are primary buyers or an awareness market.

## Price Affects Brand Signals

Price changes what customers expect.

- A low price can make a product feel accessible, but may weaken a premium claim.
- A high price can support quality positioning, but requires stronger evidence.
- A donation ask depends on mission clarity and trust, not only product appeal.
- A giftable item may support a higher price if the story and quality are strong.

The builders should ask whether the brand identity makes the price feel believable.

## Brand Signals Affect Market Segment Fit

Visual identity can invite or turn away customers.

- School-pride identity may attract students, staff, families, and alumni.
- Premium minimal identity may attract adult supporters or retail shoppers.
- Bold streetwear identity may attract style-driven buyers.
- Mission-led identity may support donations or supporter purchases.

The builders should not treat design as neutral.

## Quality Affects Willingness To Pay

Quality must be visible or explainable.

- Better materials can support a higher price.
- Local production can support a higher price if customers value it.
- Strong packaging can support retail carry.
- Weak finishing can damage trust even if the story is strong.

Example:

If a product is 100% Detroit-made, both builders should ask what evidence supports the customer's willingness to pay for local production.

## Channel Affects Design Decisions

Channel changes what the brand has to do.

- TechTown table: fast recognition, clear price, quick story.
- School launch: peer relevance, school pride, affordability.
- Phoenix Nest: retail polish, packaging, quality proof.
- Online awareness: clear photography and short copy.
- Gift buying: story, quality, and recipient appeal.

The builder outputs should reflect the channel, not just the product.

## Comparable Products Inform Both Builders

Comparable products should shape both market and brand reasoning:

- Market Fit Builder uses comparables to understand price, audience, and channel.
- Brand Fit Builder uses comparables to understand visual category, quality cues, and differentiation.

Students should identify what they are borrowing as a category signal and what makes the Renni product distinct.

## Evidence Flow Into Structured Evidence

Builder outputs should help students create structured evidence entries.

Recommended mapping:

- Segment recommendation -> claim.
- Survey/interview/comparable -> evidence.
- Source name or link -> source.
- Price tolerance or conversion expectation -> assumption.
- Weak segment fit -> risk.
- Interview, pop-up test, or stakeholder review -> next validation.

Builder entries should not replace structured evidence. They should make it easier to write better structured evidence.

## Output Flow Into Playbook Sections

Builder outputs should feed these Playbook areas:

- Ch. 5: brand identity rationale.
- Ch. 6: supporting brand fit and launch readiness.
- Ch. 7: product, price, demand, and recommendation.
- Ch. 8: revenue assumptions and pricing logic.
- Ch. 10: customer insight and campaign implications.
- Ch. 11: Phoenix Nest retail carry evidence, offer, and ask.

Students should still write the final Playbook text themselves.

## Example Interconnections

Premium adult-facing brand:

- Brand signal: mature, quiet, higher quality.
- Market question: Are students buyers, awareness audience, or validators?
- Evidence needed: adult supporter interviews, comparable pricing, Phoenix Nest feedback.

Student-accessible market:

- Market signal: lower price, peer relevance, school pride.
- Brand question: Does the identity feel too expensive or too quiet?
- Evidence needed: student survey, willingness-to-pay test, design preference test.

Detroit-made product:

- Product signal: local production and story.
- Market question: Which segment values local production enough to pay more?
- Brand question: Does the identity make local quality visible?
- Evidence needed: interviews, comparable local apparel, retail stakeholder feedback.
