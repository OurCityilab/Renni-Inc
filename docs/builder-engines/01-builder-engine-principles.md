# Builder Engine Principles

Builder engines exist to help students reason. They should make the logic behind a decision easier to see, test, and turn into Playbook-ready work.

## Core Principle

Every student decision sends a signal.  
Every signal creates a tradeoff.  
Every tradeoff requires evidence.  
Every claim needs a validation step.  
Every final output should become Playbook-ready.

## Shared Philosophy

Builders should not reward fast guessing. They should guide students toward defensible judgment.

A good builder asks students to connect:

- What they are making.
- Who it is for.
- Why that audience would care.
- What the price or design implies.
- What evidence supports the claim.
- What still needs validation.
- How the answer should show up in the Playbook.

## Cause And Effect Logic

Builder engines should show how one choice changes the next choice.

Examples:

- If a product is expensive to make, the price point likely rises.
- If the price point rises, the target customer may change.
- If the target customer changes, the channel and message may need to change.
- If the brand looks premium, students need evidence that the intended market values that premium signal.
- If the team chooses a low-price strategy, quality, margin, and production capacity become tradeoffs.

## Tradeoff Logic

Builders should help students compare options instead of treating every choice as all upside.

A tradeoff should include:

- The choice being made.
- The benefit of that choice.
- The cost or risk of that choice.
- The evidence students have.
- The evidence students still need.
- The next validation step.

Builders should not hide weak assumptions. They should make weak assumptions easier to improve.

## Evidence Standard

Builder outputs should connect to the Structured Evidence Standard:

- Claim: what students believe is true.
- Evidence: what supports it.
- Source: where the evidence came from.
- Assumption: what is not yet proven.
- Risk: what could make the claim wrong.
- Confidence: how strong the evidence is.
- Next validation: what students should test next.

Student-authored source notes remain the source of truth. Builders can organize and interpret student-provided information, but they should not create facts.

## Validation Loop

Each builder should push students through a loop:

1. Make a working assumption.
2. Connect it to product, market, brand, price, or channel logic.
3. Attach evidence.
4. Name the risk.
5. Decide what to validate next.
6. Revise the Playbook output after validation.

This loop matters more than a polished first answer.

## Deterministic Outputs And AI Critique

Builder calculations and stored builder entries should be deterministic. If students enter the same inputs, the builder should return the same result.

AI critique has a different role:

- It can review student-authored inputs.
- It can identify missing evidence or weak assumptions.
- It can suggest revision language clearly labeled as a suggestion.
- It cannot create official answers.
- It cannot change calculations.
- It cannot write back into student work automatically.
- It cannot decide whether work is approved.

The builder is the working tool. The critique is a temporary coaching layer.

## What Builders Should Do

Builders should:

- Ask for concrete product, brand, market, price, channel, and evidence inputs.
- Show cause/effect relationships.
- Surface tradeoffs in plain language.
- Preserve uncertainty when evidence is weak.
- Prompt students to validate with real people or real data.
- Produce compact summaries students can turn into Playbook text.
- Keep final writing student-authored.

## What Builders Should Not Do

Builders should not:

- Pretend estimates are facts.
- Choose the final strategy for students.
- Replace source notes.
- Replace structured evidence.
- Write final Playbook text automatically.
- Change submit gates or readiness.
- Change approval, task, deliverable, or blocked status.
- Create payment, checkout, refund, tax, or inventory-decrement features.
- Pull broad unrelated data into a section.
- Add complexity that students cannot explain.

## Review Standard For Future Builders

A future builder is ready only when a reviewer can answer yes to these questions:

- Does it help students understand a real tradeoff?
- Does it keep student-authored evidence as the source of truth?
- Does it avoid invented data?
- Does it preserve existing gates and readiness behavior?
- Does it produce a useful Playbook-ready summary without replacing student writing?
- Does it stay small enough for students to use during class?
