# Builder Engines

Builder engines are guided decision tools inside Renni Command Center. They help Renaissance students turn product facts, evidence, assumptions, and tradeoffs into stronger Playbook-ready work.

They are not simple forms. A form collects answers. A builder engine helps students understand what their answers imply.

## Purpose

Builder engines should help students:

- Connect brand identity, product quality, price point, target market, channel, and evidence.
- See the cause/effect chain behind a business decision.
- Compare options instead of assuming the first answer is correct.
- Identify weak assumptions before they become final claims.
- Turn working notes into professional Playbook language.
- Prepare stronger outputs for the TechTown pop-up, Brand & Operations Playbook, and Phoenix Nest retail carry pitch.

## How Builders Differ From Forms And Calculators

A form asks, "What is your target market?"

A builder asks:

- What product facts support that market?
- Does the price fit that market's purchasing power?
- Does the brand identity attract that market?
- Can the team actually reach that market through the planned channel?
- What evidence proves or challenges the assumption?
- What should students validate next?

A calculator returns a number. A builder should show the reasoning behind the number and the risk behind the assumption.

## Learning Role

Builder engines should make professional thinking visible. Students should learn that decisions are connected:

brand identity -> design/style -> quality -> price point -> target market -> evidence -> channel -> inventory/revenue decision -> Playbook-ready positioning

The goal is not to force a single answer. The goal is to help students calibrate the answer they choose.

## Relationship To Existing Product Spine

Builder engines should work inside the current Renni Command Center spine:

- Template Studios define the chapter sections, requirements, prompts, and optional builder visibility.
- Deliverable Output Workspace is where students write source notes, draft responses, final Playbook text, evidence links, structured evidence, and builder entries.
- Structured Evidence captures claims, evidence, assumptions, risks, confidence, and validation steps.
- Market Builder and future builders provide deterministic student-authored reasoning support.
- AI critique can review student-authored inputs, but it remains temporary, no-write, and non-authoritative.

## Guardrails

Builder engines must preserve the current system boundaries:

- No model-based approval.
- No automatic submission.
- No invented data.
- No replacement for student source notes.
- No automatic changes to final Playbook text.
- No changes to submit gates.
- No changes to Playbook readiness.
- No changes to approval, task status, deliverable status, or blocked behavior.
- No point-of-sale, checkout, refund, tax, or inventory-decrement behavior.
- Square remains the external point-of-sale system.

## V1 Builder Family Direction

The near-term builder family should stay small:

- Market Fit Builder: helps students compare possible markets and evidence strength.
- Brand Fit Builder: helps students understand how identity choices send market signals.
- Existing Market Builder: supports demand and scenario assumptions.
- Future Decision Memo Builder: should come later, after market and brand reasoning are stable.

The priority is depth of reasoning, not number of tools.
