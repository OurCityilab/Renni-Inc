# Student-Facing Language Bank

Use this language when adding builder prompts, helper text, empty states, warnings, and critique copy. Keep the tone direct, student-friendly, and focused on action.

## General Helper Text

- "Use this builder to test your thinking before you write final Playbook text."
- "Start with what you know. Mark the rest as assumptions."
- "A strong answer connects product facts, customer fit, evidence, and next steps."
- "If the evidence is weak, say that clearly and decide how to validate it."
- "This tool supports your decision. It does not make the decision for you."

## Tradeoff Prompts

- "What do we gain by choosing this direction?"
- "What do we risk by choosing this direction?"
- "Who becomes more likely to buy?"
- "Who becomes less likely to buy?"
- "Does this choice support the price point?"
- "Does this choice make production easier or harder?"
- "Does this choice fit the channel where customers will see it?"

## Evidence Prompts

- "What evidence supports this claim?"
- "Where did this evidence come from?"
- "Is this based on real feedback, observation, comparable products, or a guess?"
- "What would make this claim stronger?"
- "What evidence would prove this assumption wrong?"
- "What source should be linked before this becomes final Playbook text?"

## Validation Prompts

- "What should we test next?"
- "Who should we ask?"
- "What question should we ask them?"
- "What result would increase confidence?"
- "What result would make us change direction?"
- "Can this be tested at school, TechTown, Phoenix Nest, or through interviews?"

## Empty States

- "Add source notes before using this builder."
- "No market assumptions have been saved yet."
- "No brand signals have been described yet."
- "Start with one product and one possible customer."
- "Add at least one piece of evidence before calling this a recommendation."
- "This section has no builder entries yet. Use the prompts above to begin."

## Warnings

- "Do not treat an estimate as a fact."
- "A popular product is not always a profitable product."
- "A market can be easy to reach but weak at paying."
- "A high price needs stronger evidence."
- "A strong story still needs customer proof."
- "A design that looks premium may not fit every audience."
- "A design that students like may not be enough for retail carry."
- "People wearing a brand does not prove they are the strategic buyer for that brand."
- "Affordability data can support context, but it does not prove demand."
- "Total market is not the same as reachable market or likely buyers."

## AI Critique Guardrail Language

- "The coach reviews your evidence and assumptions. It does not create official answers."
- "Suggestions are not final Playbook text."
- "Student source notes remain the source of truth."
- "Verify every number, source, and claim before using a suggestion."
- "Chiefs and instructor feedback remain the final review path."
- "If evidence is missing, add evidence instead of treating a suggestion as proof."

## Market Fit Builder Examples

For a premium sweatshirt:

- "A $100 sweatshirt may need a buyer with more spending power than the average student."
- "Students may still be the best awareness audience, even if they are not the primary buyer."
- "Test whether parents, alumni, Detroit supporters, or gift buyers value the local production story."
- "Compare this price to similar premium sweatshirts before calling it realistic."

For a $20 t-shirt:

- "This price may fit students better, but the design still has to feel worth choosing."
- "A lower price can help volume, but the team still needs to check margin."
- "Ask whether students would buy for themselves, for school pride, or only if the design is strong."

For baked goods:

- "Freshness, taste, price, and table placement may matter more than long brand story."
- "Use samples, quick feedback, and repeat-interest questions to test demand."
- "The strongest market may change by location and time of day."

For donations:

- "A donation is not the same as a product sale."
- "People need to understand the mission and trust where the money goes."
- "Parents, alumni, staff, and supporters may be stronger donation audiences than students."

## Product Scan Copy

- "What kind of product does this behave like: basic merch, premium merch, streetwear, local-made apparel, giftable product, food impulse buy, or supporter ask?"
- "What product facts make the price believable?"
- "What quality proof would a customer need before paying this price?"
- "What does the made-in or local story change about the likely buyer?"
- "What channel makes this product easiest to understand and buy?"
- "What production constraint could change the market or price?"

## Comparable Product Scan Copy

- "This comp is useful for product category, but weak for price."
- "This comp is useful for premium positioning, but weak for school connection."
- "Find one comp that matches the product and one comp that matches the customer."
- "A basic school hoodie is not enough evidence for a premium Detroit-made sweatshirt."
- "A brand students wear can be a style reference without proving students are the target buyer."
- "Name what this comp proves and what it does not prove."

## Source Coach Copy

- "What source would make this claim stronger?"
- "Use a survey when you need student preference or student price reaction."
- "Use adult interviews when the price may be beyond regular student spending."
- "Use Census or ACS data for affordability context, not proof of demand."
- "Use comparable products to test whether the price and positioning are realistic."
- "Use retail observation when the product needs to work in Phoenix Nest or at a table."
- "Use a preorder or interest test before making a high-risk inventory call."

## Customer Profile Naming Copy

- "This looks like a School Spirit Buyer if school identity is the main reason to buy."
- "This looks like a Proud Parent Supporter if family pride and support are stronger than student spending power."
- "This looks like an Alumni Legacy Buyer if school connection and giving back matter."
- "This looks like a Civic Premium Buyer if Detroit-made quality and local pride support the price."
- "This looks like a Gift Buyer if the product is purchased for someone else."
- "This looks like a Premium Casualwear Buyer if quality, fit, and style matter more than school affiliation."
- "This looks like a Mission Supporter / Donor if the purchase is mostly about supporting the student enterprise."

## Affordability, Demand, And Market Size Copy

- "Someone being able to afford the product does not prove they want it."
- "Someone liking the product does not prove they will buy it at this price."
- "Total market means everyone who might fit the profile."
- "Reachable market means the people this team can actually reach through the channel."
- "Interested market means the people likely to care after seeing the product and story."
- "Likely buyers means the people expected to convert at the proposed price."
- "Do not use total market as likely buyers."

## Brand Fit Builder Examples

For color:

- "Bright colors can create energy and recognition, but may feel less premium if overused."
- "Muted colors can feel mature and higher quality, but may feel too quiet for a student launch."

For fonts:

- "A varsity font can signal school pride, but may not fit a premium retail pitch."
- "A serif font can feel established, but may feel too formal for a youth-focused product."
- "A gothic font can feel bold and cultural, but may hurt readability."

For logo direction:

- "A complex logo can tell more story, but may fail when embroidered or viewed small."
- "A simple icon can work across products, but may need stronger supporting copy."

For production:

- "Embroidery can support quality, but it limits detail and increases cost."
- "Screen print can support bold graphics, but color count and placement affect production."
- "A patch can feel premium, but it adds cost and production steps."

## Playbook-Ready Transition Phrases

- "The strongest current evidence suggests..."
- "The main assumption still needing validation is..."
- "This market appears promising because..."
- "The main tradeoff is..."
- "The team should validate this by..."
- "This brand direction supports the target market by..."
- "This price point is strongest if..."
- "The next evidence source should be..."

## Review Questions Before Finalizing

- "Can another student explain this decision using the evidence?"
- "Would a chief understand the tradeoff?"
- "Would Phoenix Nest understand why this product fits retail?"
- "Would a customer understand the value quickly?"
- "Does the final Playbook text separate facts from assumptions?"
- "Is the next validation step specific enough to act on?"
