# Launch Morning — Student Quick Start

Paste into Canvas / Google Classroom. Keep it on the projector
while students work.

## What you are running

**Renni Inc.** is the parent company. **House Phoenix** is the
flagship brand (supporting brands: Lumen, Notice, Humble Oven).
Products: beanies, sweatshirts, t-shirts, baked goods, donations.

This week the company is producing three things:

1. The **TechTown pop-up** — a real sales / test channel.
2. The **Brand & Operations Playbook** — the document the next
   cohort will inherit.
3. The **Phoenix Nest retail carry pitch** — proposal to a real
   retail buyer.

## Daily flow (every student)

1. Open **My Next Actions** on the home dashboard.
2. Open the assigned section.
3. Use the **builder / table / checklist** at the top of the section
   first — so you don't start from a blank page.
4. **Copy** the output into Working Draft.
5. **Edit it in your own words.** The draft has to sound like your
   team, not the builder.
6. **Save.**
7. Tell your chief it is ready to review.

## If you are stuck

- Use the builder anyway. If your numbers are guesses, label them
  assumptions and note what would make you certain.
- Ask the chief on the section. If the chief is also stuck, bring
  it to a Co-CEO.

## Finance sections (Ch. 7 / Ch. 8)

Use the **Finance Table** at the top of each section.

- **Unit Cost Table** — list every product. Add the costs that exist
  only because we sold the unit. Total unit cost is calculated.
- **Break-Even Table** — unit cost, sale price, fixed cost / goal.
  The table calculates margin and units to break even. If margin is
  negative, you'll get a warning: the price is too low to break even.
- **Revenue Scenarios Table** — low / target / stretch per product.
  Revenue = quantity × price.
- **Donation Scenarios Table** — donor type × count × average gift.
  Donations are recorded separately from product sales.
- **KPI Table** — 4–7 numbers leaders watch. Each needs a formula,
  target, source, owner, and review rhythm.

After the table is honest, copy it into Working Draft and write the
2–3 sentence explanation underneath.

## Operations sections (Ch. 9)

Use the **Operations Checklist** at the top of each section.

- **Inventory Checklist** — every item we bring: quantity, location,
  owner, issue / risk, and packed?
- **Day-of SOP** — the day in time order. Each step has an owner,
  materials, done signal, and a backup person.
- **Baked Goods SOP** — every step that touches food. Read the
  banner: it is **not legal food-safety advice.** Follow school,
  event, and instructor rules.
- **Continuity Checklist** — what the next cohort needs to pick up
  where we left off. Status, owner, link / location, warning,
  next step.

## BMC sections (Ch. 4)

- **Customer Segments** — use the Customer Profile Builder. Compose
  the profile with the chips, then draft it in your own words. Add a
  quote, observation, or labeled assumption as proof.
- **Key Activities** — pick the 5–7 activities Renni Inc. has to do
  well as a real retail company. NOT just pop-up tasks. Use the Key
  Activities Builder and explain why each one matters.

## For chiefs and Co-CEOs

Open **C-suite → Project Navigator** before class.

- Look at **blocked**, **overdue**, and **ready** tasks.
- Pick the most launch-critical section your team owns.
- Review the latest Working Draft. If the section uses a builder,
  check that the table / checklist / SOP underneath makes the same
  case the draft does.
- If a draft is generic, ask: "What would the customer or the next
  cohort need to know that this draft doesn't say yet?"

## Reminders

- **Renni Inc.** is the business. **House Phoenix** is the flagship
  brand. **TechTown** is a sales / test channel. **Phoenix Nest** is
  a separate retail carry channel.
- Use **Playbook**, not Bible. Use **Strategy and Growth**, not R&D.
- Renni Command Center is **not a POS.** Square is the external POS.
- Builders **do not save anything** by themselves — only your save
  in Working Draft counts.
