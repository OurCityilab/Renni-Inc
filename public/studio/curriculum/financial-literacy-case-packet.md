# MONEY IN REAL LIFE
## Financial Literacy Simulation Week — Student Case Packet

**Our City / Rose City Studio**

This week is not a worksheet. You'll get a paycheck, a budget, a credit decision, and a shot at your own place — on paper, with your team, before life makes you do it for real. The Studio modules are your checkpoints: **do the case first, then save your work in Studio.**

One rule all week: no shame, no fake flexing. Everybody's numbers are practice numbers.

# DAY 1 CASE — The GDYT Paycheck Case
### "Your first check is not your whole check."

**The setup.** You landed a summer job through Grow Detroit's Young Talent. The offer:

- **Pay:** $15.50/hour
- **Hours:** 21 hours/week
- **Length:** 6 weeks

**Round 1 — Guess before you math.**
Write down your gut answer, no calculator:

1. Total money you'll EARN this summer (gross): $________
2. Total money that will actually HIT YOUR ACCOUNT (take-home): $________

**Round 2 — Do the math with your team.**

- Gross for the summer = wage × hours × weeks = $________
- Take-home estimate = about 75% of gross (taxes and deductions come out first) = $________
- Take-home per week = $________

**Round 3 — The First-Check Plan.**
Your real weekly costs. Decide as a team what your plan covers:

| Cost | Your number |
|---|---|
| Getting to work (bus pass, gas money, rides) | $ |
| Food/snacks on work days | $ |
| Phone / personal | $ |
| Helping out at home (your call — real option, not a punishment) | $ |
| Savings goal for the summer | $ |
| What's left to spend | $ |

**Round 4 — Surprise card.** Your coach will hand your team an event card. It happens THIS week. Rewrite the plan. What moved? What didn't you touch?

**Deliverable:** *My First Check Plan* (this sheet, revised after the surprise card).
**Studio checkpoint:** Open **Take-Home Pay** in the Financial Literacy Lab, enter your case numbers, and save your reflection.
**Exit ticket:** "What did you think was yours that actually wasn't?"

# DAY 2 CASE — The $1,300 Month
### "A budget is not a spreadsheet. It's a set of decisions."

**The setup.** You're a year out of school, working part-time with shifting hours. Your take-home this month is **$1,300**. You live with family but carry your own weight.

**Round 1 — Build the budget.**

| Category | Your plan |
|---|---|
| Transportation (bus pass / gas / rides) | $ |
| Phone | $ |
| Food (groceries + eating out — split it if you want) | $ |
| Clothes | $ |
| Helping at home | $ |
| Fun / entertainment / subscriptions | $ |
| Savings | $ |
| **Total (must equal $1,300)** | **$1,300** |

**Rounds 2 and 3 — Event cards.** Two cards will hit your team this "month." After each one, rebudget. The total is still $1,300. Something has to give — your team decides what, and writes one sentence on WHY.

**Round 4 — Team defense.** Prepare a 60-second defense of your final budget:
1. What we protected no matter what, and why.
2. What we cut, and why it was the right cut.
3. What we'd do differently with $200 more.

**Gallery walk:** post your budget. Read the other teams'. Leave one sticky note per team: something smart you saw, or a risk they might be missing. ("Hard to picture how this covers food" is fair. "That's dumb" is not.)

**Deliverable:** *My Budget Defense* (final budget + the three defense answers).
**Studio checkpoint:** Open **Budget Builder**, enter YOUR split, and save *My Budget Plan*.

# DAY 3 CASES — Credit Can Help You or Trap You
### "Looks affordable" and "is affordable" are two different numbers.

Four short cases. Your team sorts each one: **SMART / RISKY / TRAP** — and you have to say why using the actual numbers.

**Case A — The Sneaker Split.** $180 sneakers, "4 easy payments of $45." Miss one payment: $17 late fee and the app reports it. You have $60 in your account and get paid in 9 days.

**Case B — The Secured Card.** You put down a $200 deposit, get a $200-limit card. You put your $40 phone bill on it every month and pay it off in full. Six months later, you have a payment history. Nobody hyped this to you. It's boring. That's the point.

**Case C — The Lot on the Corner.** A $4,500 car, financed at **24% interest for 48 months**. Payment "only $148/month." Total paid over 4 years: about **$7,100** for a $4,500 car — if it never breaks down. You need a car to get to a better job.

**Case D — The Emergency.** Your family needs $350 by Friday. Options: (1) put it on a credit card at 27% and pay $25/month, (2) borrow from a payday spot for a $60 fee per two weeks, (3) use your $400 emergency savings, (4) pick up extra shifts but the money lands after Friday. Choose and defend.

**The Minimum Payment Trap (whole room demo).** $600 balance, 27% interest, $20 minimum payments. Watch how long it takes and what it really costs. Guess first: months ________ total paid $________

**Team debate:** *"Is credit bad?"* Half the room argues each side — with numbers, not vibes. Then everyone drops the act and we write the real answer together.

**Rewrite the rule.** Finish this sentence as a team, your words: *"Before I sign up to pay later, I will always ________."*

**Deliverable:** *My Credit Rulebook* — your team's 5 rules for using credit without getting used.
**Studio checkpoint:** Open **Credit & Debt**, run your numbers, save your reflection.

# DAY 4 CASE — The First Apartment Decision
### "Could you really move out?"

**The setup.** You're 19, working full-time at $16/hour. Monthly take-home ≈ **$2,080** (75% of gross). You want out. Here are your four paths — every team gets assigned one to build, then you argue for the one you'd actually pick.

**Path 1 — Stay home and stack.** Pay $200/month at home, save aggressively. Boring. Powerful.
**Path 2 — Roommates.** $1,150 two-bedroom split two ways, plus utilities split. Roommate risk is real (see event cards).
**Path 3 — Move too fast.** Solo one-bedroom at $950. Landlord wants income 3× rent — do you even qualify? Check the math.
**Path 4 — The alternative.** Don't move yet. Put the money toward a car, a certification, or starting something — the thing that raises your income FIRST.

**What the landlord checks (role play).** One student is the landlord. Applicants bring: income (3× rent rule), credit history, deposit money, and — if the numbers are thin — a co-signer, a bigger deposit, or a letter showing you've paid on time before. The landlord asks real questions. No shame — if the application is thin, the question is *"what would make this a yes in 6 months?"*

**Move-in math (the part nobody posts about):**

| Cost | Estimate |
|---|---|
| First month's rent | $ |
| Security deposit (often = 1 month, sometimes 1.5) | $ |
| Utilities setup + first month (electric, gas, internet) | $ |
| Furniture minimum (bed, something to sit on, plates) | $ |
| Food for the first month | $ |
| Transportation | $ |
| Emergency fund you should NOT touch | $ |
| **Cash you need on day one** | **$** |

**The 12-Month Money Plan.** Whatever path you pick: map the next 12 months. Savings target per month, credit moves, income moves, and the date you could hand a landlord a strong application — or the date you hit your alternative goal instead.

**Deliverable:** *12-Month Money Plan Pitch* — 2 minutes, every teammate speaks.
**Studio checkpoints:** Save your work in **Renting & Ownership**, then build **My Money Plan** — that's your whole week in one document.

# EVENT CARDS
*(Coach cuts these apart. When a card hits, it's real — rebudget.)*

1. **Cracked screen.** Your phone screen shatters. Repair: $120, or live with it and risk the phone dying.
2. **Bus pass.** Your ride situation fell through. You need a $70 monthly pass starting now.
3. **The borrow.** A close friend asks to borrow $60. They still owe you $20 from last time.
4. **Family needs help.** Someone at home is short $100 on a bill this month. Nobody made you offer. Do you?
5. **Missed payment fee.** A payment slipped your mind. $35 fee, and it may get reported.
6. **Hours cut.** Slow week — your check is $80 lighter this period.
7. **Extra shift.** You can pick up a shift worth $90, but it's the same night as something you care about.
8. **Interview fit.** You landed an interview for something better. You need clothes that say so: ~$75.
9. **Car trouble.** The car you (or your ride) depend on needs a $220 repair.
10. **Sneaky subscription.** A free trial you forgot became $14.99/month — three months ago.
11. **Rent increase.** (Day 4 teams) Renewal letter: rent goes up $75/month.
12. **Roommate short.** (Day 4 teams) Your roommate has half their half. Rent is due in 5 days.
13. **Deposit required.** The utility company wants a $150 deposit because you have no payment history.
14. **Credit check bounce.** Application denied: score too low / no history. You need a co-signer, a bigger deposit, or 6 more months of on-time payments.
15. **The save.** Your emergency fund covers a surprise bill completely. Feel that. Write down what it would have cost you WITHOUT the fund (fees, interest, stress).
16. **Pay-upfront discount.** A certification course you want is $300 — or $200 if you can pay in full today. Can your plan take the hit for the discount?

# ROLE CARDS
*(One per team member or one per team — play the hand you're dealt.)*

1. **Steady 25.** Reliable job, 25 hrs/week, same schedule every week. Predictable but capped.
2. **Holding it down.** You contribute $150/month at home, non-negotiable, and you're proud of it. Budget around it.
3. **Car dreamer.** Saving for a $3,000 car by spring. Every decision runs through that filter.
4. **Ready to launch.** Trying to move out within 12 months. Deposit, credit, income — you're building all three.
5. **Whiplash hours.** Some weeks 28 hours, some weeks 11. Your income is a range, not a number.
6. **Backed but building.** Family covers essentials — this money is fully yours. So what's your excuse for no plan?
7. **The commute.** No car, job is two buses away. Transportation eats time AND money — protect it first.
8. **Discipline, no paper.** You never miss, never borrow — but you have zero credit history, and the system can't see you yet.
9. **Approved and tempted.** You have a card with a $500 limit and a habit of tapping it for wants. The limit is not the budget.
